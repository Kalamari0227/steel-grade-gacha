# Steel Grade Gacha — Character Design Bible v0.1

This document defines the visual system for the 34 steel-grade characters. The goal is not to make 34 unrelated illustrations, but one coherent material universe where the visual language helps players remember steel-family behavior, strength progression, and process characteristics.

## 1. Core rule

Every character is generated from three layers:

1. **Family identity** — PO / CR / GA
2. **Strength tier** — 270 → 340/370/440 → 590/780 → 980/1180 → 1470/PHS
3. **Mechanism trait** — drawing quality, BH, W, Y, martensitic, hot press, coating/protection

The silhouette should communicate strength tier first, family second, and mechanism trait third.

## 2. Shared visual grammar

- **Card art ratio:** 2:3 portrait
- **Character framing:** full body or 3/4 body, readable silhouette, clear head/shoulder outline
- **Background:** family-specific environment, but always secondary to the character
- **Weapons/props:** only when they encode material behavior
- **Armor:** represents strength and structural rigidity
- **Flexible cloth/joints:** represents formability
- **Surface sheen/coating:** represents finishing and coating behavior
- **Heat/glow:** reserved for bake hardening, PHS, and heat-treatment transformations
- **Do not equate rarity with technical superiority.** Rarity is a game layer; character evolution should follow material behavior.

## 3. The three houses

### PO — Hot-Rolled / Pickled House

**Theme:** furnace, scale, raw power, industrial forge, rolling mill.

**Palette:** charcoal, oxide black, burnt orange, dark red, gunmetal.

**Silhouette:** broader body, heavier boots/gauntlets, more visible plates and structural beams.

**Texture:** matte steel, mill scale, brushed hot-rolled surface, heat scars.

**Personality:** direct, durable, powerful, practical.

**Signature motif:** forge marks / rolled edge / heated steel glow.

**Visual message:** "Strength born in heat."

### CR — Cold-Rolled House

**Theme:** precision, control, smooth surface, dimensional accuracy, engineered movement.

**Palette:** silver, white steel, cool blue, graphite, faint cyan.

**Silhouette:** slimmer, balanced, cleaner geometry, articulated joints.

**Texture:** smooth cold-rolled steel, polished edges, precision-machined details.

**Personality:** disciplined, technical, agile, exact.

**Signature motif:** precision lines / calibration marks / fine segmented armor.

**Visual message:** "Strength under control."

### GA — Galvannealed / Coated House

**Theme:** protection, barrier, corrosion defense, layered armor.

**Palette:** pale zinc silver, cool gray, soft gold, blue-gray, white.

**Silhouette:** CR-derived body architecture plus an obvious external defensive layer.

**Texture:** zinc crystal / matte metallic coating / layered plated armor.

**Personality:** protective, resilient, dependable, defensive.

**Signature motif:** shield plates / hexagonal zinc pattern / protective aura.

**Visual message:** "Strength that protects."

## 4. Strength evolution system

### Tier 1 — 270 class: Base / Formability

**Role:** civilians, scouts, artisans, acrobats, flexible support characters.

- Light armor
- Open joints
- More fabric and flexible elements
- Friendly or calm expression
- Rounded silhouette
- Smaller weapons or no weapon

The character should look easy to move and form, not weak or useless.

### Tier 2 — 340 / 370 / 440 class: Reinforced

**Role:** trained guard, specialist, junior knight.

- More shoulder/chest structure
- First rigid armor plates
- Stronger stance
- Still visibly flexible
- Family motif becomes more obvious

This is the transition point between formability-first and strength-first identities.

### Tier 3 — 590 / 780 class: Combat

**Role:** frontline fighter / structural guardian.

- Strong triangular silhouette
- Larger gauntlets, chest frame, greaves
- Stronger posture and motion
- Visible mechanical or phase-change motifs for Y grades
- More aggressive background energy

The 780 class should look clearly more structural and crash-oriented than 590.

### Tier 4 — 980 / 1180 class: UHSS Elite

**Role:** elite knight, royal guard, exoskeleton warrior.

- Dense armor
- Narrower exposed flexible zones
- Reinforced spine, shoulders, hips
- Darker or more intense material contrast
- Less casual mobility in the pose

These characters should visually communicate the tradeoff: outstanding strength but reduced ease of forming.

### Tier 5 — 1470 / PHS: Final / Transformation

**Role:** legendary guardian, boss-class unit, awakened form.

- Nearly complete armor architecture
- Iconic silhouette recognizable at thumbnail size
- Very limited exposed soft elements
- Heat-treatment transformation effects when relevant
- Should feel process-dependent, not simply "bigger"

1470/PHS is a transformation endpoint, not just another incremental tier.

## 5. Suffix and mechanism language

### C / D / E / F / S-EDDQ — Drawing/formability lineage

These should be designed as a **mobility lineage**, not a strength ladder.

- C: balanced baseline
- D: more flexible joints and cloth
- E: deeper articulation / ribbon-like or spring-like movement
- F / S-EDDQ: maximum flexibility, elegant flowing silhouette, least rigid visual mass

The higher drawing-quality versions should not look "more powerful"; they should look more capable of complex movement and deformation.

### W — Solid-solution / strengthened general-purpose type

Use dense internal frame elements without making the character rigid.

Motifs:
- reinforced ribs
- lattice structures
- compact power core
- subtle alloy crystal patterns

### H — Bake Hardenable

Core concept: **latent strength / heat-triggered awakening**.

Normal appearance:
- relatively smooth, formable armor
- dormant heat lines

Awakened appearance:
- heated seams glow
- armor locks into a harder configuration
- shield or body frame expands

The character should visually teach: form first, strengthen after paint-bake heat.

### Y — Transformation-strengthened / multiphase identity

Core concept: **multiple internal phases working together**.

Motifs:
- two-tone or multi-material armor
- hard islands embedded in a more flexible matrix
- segmented armor with contrasting cores
- asymmetric but controlled structure

590Y should retain visible flexibility. 780Y becomes more frame-like. 980Y and 1180Y become increasingly armor-dominant.

### M — Martensitic

Core concept: **maximum hardness and direct structural force**.

Motifs:
- sharp faceted armor
- blade-like geometry
- dark high-contrast metal
- minimal soft articulation

CR 1470M should feel like a fully hardened final knight.

### PHS / Hot Press

Core concept: **transformation through heat and die quenching**.

Required two-state storytelling:
- before heat treatment: simpler, more formable, incomplete armor
- after heat treatment: hardened final guardian form

PO PHS and GA Al-Si hot press should share this transformation concept but preserve family identity.

## 6. Cross-family twin rule

Characters with the same grade strength across different families should look related, not identical.

Example: CR 780Y and GA 780Y

- Same basic body architecture / combat tier
- Same Y multiphase motif
- GA adds visible protective coating layer, shield plating, or zinc-pattern surface
- CR remains cleaner, more precise, and more exposed

This creates a "same class, different house" feeling and helps players connect metallurgy concepts.

## 7. Prototype lineup — first five characters to redesign

These five establish the full visual grammar before scaling to all 34 characters.

### A. PO 270C — baseline

**Purpose:** define the PO family at the lowest strength tier.

Design:
- young forge scout / mill worker guardian
- charcoal work armor
- warm orange edge glow
- broad boots, simple gauntlets
- minimal chest plate
- relaxed but sturdy stance

Key teaching signal: basic hot-rolled/pickled steel, useful and formable, not elite armor.

### B. CR 440W — precision specialist

**Purpose:** define CR visual language and the transition into reinforced grades.

Design:
- slim precision knight
- silver segmented armor
- cool-blue calibration lines
- compact reinforced ribs around torso
- articulated knees and shoulders

Key teaching signal: stronger than base drawing grades while still controlled and usable.

### C. GA 780Y — coated frontline guardian

**Purpose:** establish GA + Y + combat tier.

Design:
- frontline shield guardian
- silver-gray layered coating armor
- faint zinc crystal texture
- two-phase inner core visible through armor gaps
- strong side-impact style shield structure

Key teaching signal: high-strength multiphase steel plus protective surface identity.

### D. CR 1180Y — UHSS elite

**Purpose:** establish the elite tier and strength/formability tradeoff.

Design:
- narrow-waist exoskeleton warrior
- dense silver/graphite upper frame
- visible hard phase cores
- reduced flexible joint area
- powerful but controlled stance, not acrobatic

Key teaching signal: exceptional strength with visibly constrained mobility/formability.

### E. CR 1470M — final martensitic form

**Purpose:** define the top-end non-PHS final form.

Design:
- fully armored martensitic knight
- sharp faceted dark-silver armor
- almost no fabric
- blade-like shoulder and forearm geometry
- cold, high-contrast lighting

Key teaching signal: maximum hardened structural identity.

## 8. First-pass production workflow

1. Generate the five prototype characters above at 2:3 ratio.
2. Review silhouette consistency at thumbnail scale.
3. Compare family identity without text labels.
4. Compare strength progression without knowing the grades.
5. Revise until both are readable.
6. Lock rendering style, face treatment, line density, background complexity, and card crop.
7. Expand to all 34 characters.

## 9. Acceptance test

Before a design is approved, ask:

- Can a viewer guess PO / CR / GA from appearance alone?
- Can a viewer roughly rank 270 vs 780 vs 1180 vs 1470 from silhouette alone?
- Does the character communicate its mechanism without relying only on text?
- Does stronger steel look stronger without implying it is universally better?
- Does the character still belong to the same universe as the other 33?

If the answer to any of these is no, revise before production.

## 10. Asset rule

Keep the existing stable game keys unchanged (`poo-*`, `ssial-*`, `ga-*`).

Recommended future paths:

```text
assets/characters/
  original/
  redesign/
    v01/
    v02/
  character_map.csv
  CHARACTER_DESIGN_BIBLE.md
```

The final application can migrate from Base64 to external assets later without changing gameplay IDs or save-state logic.
